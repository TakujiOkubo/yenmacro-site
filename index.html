The tide is turning for Japan

For two decades Japan was defined by deflation. That era is ending, and the forces that shaped Japan's economy and markets are turning with it. Japan is going through a regime change. Inflation has returned, the Bank of Japan is normalizing its policy rate and its balance sheet, and JGB yields have moved from the old normal of the last thirty years to a new one. Japan has a fiscal problem, not a fiscal crisis. Its debt is long-dated, much of it at low fixed coupons, and backed by large financial assets. The next big story is a rebalancing of Japanese capital toward the yen, as pension funds, banks and households find a return on yen assets again.

After twenty years of deflation, we are excited to see Japan entering a new era. Yen Macro Research analyses Japan's economy, monetary policy and markets with data and economic theory. Where official statistics stop short, we build our own data series and models, and we read Japanese primary sources first-hand. Japan cannot be understood apart from a global order that is now in flux. Trade wars, the credibility of the Federal Reserve, the dollar's role in world reserves and China's place in Asian supply chains all bear on Japan, and Japan's capital in turn moves markets abroad. Our aim is to explain Japan in that global and geopolitical context, and to explain the world from Japan.

Our view

November 2026

Deflation is over

Inflation reached Japan in 2022, and long-run inflation expectations have risen with it. The Bank of Japan began raising rates in 2024, its first hikes since 2007. Japan is in new territory for policy, companies and households.

Returning to the World I Never Left (18 May 2026)

JGB yields have returned to normal

The 40-year JGB yield rose from below 1% in 2022 to around 4% by mid-2026. We read the climb as a passage from artificially low yields to normal ones, driven by higher inflation expectations and the Bank of Japan's exit from bond buying. We see little sign of a fiscal risk premium.

The Long Climb in JGB Yields Is Nearly Over (20 July 2026)

Japanese capital is rebalancing toward the yen

Japan has been a provider of capital to the world since the 1980s, buying foreign securities worth 2–3% of GDP a year, and it will remain one in the long term. But the flow has always ebbed and flowed. With yen yields at 30-year highs, banks are selling foreign bonds and buying JGBs, and pension funds have reason to hold more yen bonds. In our view, a rebalancing toward the yen has begun.
