The tide is turning for Japan

For two decades Japan was defined by deflation. That era is ending, and the forces that shaped Japan's economy and markets are turning with it. Inflation has returned, the Bank of Japan is raising rates, JGB yields are back at normal levels, and Japanese money is starting to come home. I spent twenty years covering deflationary Japan, and it is this turning point that brings me back as an economist.

Takuji Okubo, Managing Director and Chief Economist

Our view

November 2026

Deflation is over

Inflation reached Japan in 2022, and long-run inflation expectations have risen with it. The Bank of Japan began raising rates in 2024, its first hikes since 2007. Japan is in new territory for policy, companies and households.

Returning to the World I Never Left (18 May 2026)

JGB yields have returned to normal

The 40-year JGB yield rose from below 1% in 2022 to around 4% by mid-2026. I read the climb as a passage from artificially low yields to normal ones, driven by higher inflation expectations and the Bank of Japan's exit from bond buying. I see little sign of a fiscal risk premium.

The Long Climb in JGB Yields Is Nearly Over (20 July 2026)

Japanese money is starting to come home

Since the 1980s Japan has bought foreign securities worth 2–3% of GDP a year. With yen yields at 30-year highs, banks are selling foreign bonds and buying JGBs, and pension funds have reason to hold more yen bonds. In my view, yen repatriation in 2026–27 is a question of how large, not whether.

About

Takuji Okubo

Managing Director and Chief Economist

Takuji Okubo launched Yen Macro Research in 2026.

He began covering the Japanese economy in 1997 as a sell-side economist at Goldman Sachs and Merrill Lynch, and was Chief Economist for Japan at Société Générale. In 2012 he founded Japan Macro Advisors, an independent research firm.

He then joined The Economist Group as Director for Northeast Asia, in charge of the Economist Corporate Network in Japan and South Korea. He was later Global Head of Thought Leadership at NEC, and Country Representative and then Senior Advisor at G3.

He studied economics at the University of Tokyo, holds an MBA from INSEAD, and spent three years in the doctoral programme in finance at Universitat Pompeu Fabra, Barcelona.
